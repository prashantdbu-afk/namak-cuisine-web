# Namak food photography style guide

Owner-approved source photographs must remain honest representations of the served food. Production preparation may rotate, crop, resize, remove metadata, and apply restrained per-image exposure, white-balance, contrast, saturation, or sharpening corrections. It must never add or remove food, ingredients, garnish, steam, vessels, or portions, and must never use generative fill or background replacement.

## A — Ivory Plate (`ivory-plate`)

Use for amuse-bouche, EMBERS VEG, EMBERS NON-VEG, dry starters, tandoor dishes, kebabs, selected desserts, and signature cards. Prefer warm ivory, off-white, pale gray, or muted ceramic with light stone, warm ivory, or soft-gray backgrounds. A restrained banana leaf is acceptable. Favor a 45-degree view; use overhead only for naturally symmetrical compositions. Avoid large decorative chargers except for a deliberate editorial image.

## B — Indian Vessel (`indian-vessel`)

Use for curries, gravy-based mains, soups, biryani, pulao, dal, and future main-course photography. Prefer an authentic kadhai, handi, clay pot, matte ceramic curry bowl, or shallow neutral bowl. Favor a 35–45-degree view for curries and an overhead or slight angle for rice. Never digitally move existing food into a different vessel; use a strong current photograph selectively or add it to the reshoot list.

## C — Bread Basket (`bread-basket`)

Use only for naan, roti, paratha, bread baskets, and related breads. Prefer a neutral basket, restrained black-and-cream liner, warm ivory plate, and close texture at overhead or shallow 45 degrees. The checkered liner must not appear outside the bread family.

## Color and surface selection

- Orange, red, and tandoori food: ivory, pale gray, light stone, or muted sage-gray. Avoid orange/red plates, orange wood, and heavy warm grading.
- Green food: ivory, warm white, pale gray, or selected muted charcoal. Avoid green plate + green food + full green-leaf background and excessive saturation.
- Yellow, cream, and coconut food: muted charcoal, blue-gray, dark teal-gray, contrasting neutral ceramic, or an authentic coconut vessel. Maintain shadow separation.
- Brown and charred food: ivory, soft stone, pale gray, or restrained banana leaf. Avoid dark-on-dark arrangements without excellent directional light.
- Biryani and rice: clay pot, beige bowl, neutral ceramic, or occasional controlled warm wood, with raita or gravy used sparingly.

## Delivery crops

- Menu feature: 4:3, preserve the full plate and focal dish.
- Editorial/gallery: 3:2 whenever the source supports it.
- Homepage hero: 16:10 editorial mosaic rather than forced single-image enlargement.
- Mobile: 4:5 or 1:1 chosen from the original composition without cutting off essential food.

Focal points use normalized coordinates from 0 to 1. Below-the-fold images load lazily. Only the genuine homepage LCP image may load eagerly. Alt text is concise and factual, contains no price or marketing claim, and avoids unverified ingredients.

## Current processing policy

Brightness is limited to 0.94–1.08, saturation to 0.92–1.08, and WebP quality to 72–90. Hue rotation, compositing, generative editing, plate replacement, and upscaling are prohibited. A photograph needing stronger intervention is classified `reshoot`.
